Neighbourhoods · July 2026

The Port Moody neighbourhood guide, from locals.

By the Zada Group team · 8 minute read

Port Moody neighbourhoods each sell a different version of the same city: a small, inlet-wrapped community at the end of Burrard Inlet with mountains behind it and a SkyTrain line through the middle. We live and work here, so this guide is the version we give friends, not the version written from a spreadsheet.

Moody Centre.

The historic heart, and the neighbourhood changing fastest. Walkable to the station, the West Coast Express, Brewers Row, and Rocky Point Park, with a mix of older homes, new townhomes, and condos. Long-range plans concentrate growth here, which cuts both ways: more amenity and more construction. Buy here for walkability and upside; expect evolution.

Heritage Woods and Heritage Mountain.

The family benchmark. Newer executive homes up the mountain, sought-after schools, trail access out the back door, and views over the inlet on the right streets. The trade is the hill itself, and price: this is Port Moody's premium detached territory.

College Park.

One of the original neighbourhoods, tucked between the Barnet Highway and the water at the western edge of the city. Solid mid-century housing stock on generous lots, the shoreline trail nearby, and quicker access toward Burnaby and Vancouver than anywhere else in the city. Underrated, and the lots make it interesting under the new multiplex rules.

Glenayre.

Port Moody's classic starter-detached neighbourhood, sharing a border and a feel with adjacent Burquitlam. Flat streets, older ranchers and split-levels, and proximity to Burquitlam Station's growing amenity. The combination of flat serviced lots and transit proximity makes Glenayre a name you will hear often in multiplex conversations.

Pleasantside and Ioco.

The north shore of the inlet, where the city thins into forest. Character homes, deep lots, water glimpses, and the feeling of being further from the city than you are. Old Orchard Park and the Ioco townsite give it history; the trade is a car-first life and a smaller resale pool for unusual properties.

Inlet Centre, Klahanie, and Suter Brook.

The newer master-planned pocket around Suter Brook Village and Newport Village: condos and townhomes with groceries, coffee, the recreation complex, and the station in walking distance. This is Port Moody's easiest low-maintenance landing spot, popular with first-time buyers and downsizers alike.

Anmore and Belcarra, the neighbours.

Technically their own villages, practically part of the conversation. Acreage properties, estate homes, and a rural register ten minutes from SkyTrain. If privacy is the priority and the budget cooperates, this is where the search ends.

Every one of these neighbourhoods trades differently, and micro-market knowledge is most of the game here. When you are ready to look seriously, we will tell you which streets we would buy on and why.

Frequently asked questions.

Which Port Moody neighbourhood is best for families?

Heritage Woods and Heritage Mountain are the usual answer for schools, space, and trails, with College Park and Glenayre as strong value alternatives closer to the water and transit.

Where should first-time buyers look in Port Moody?

Inlet Centre, Klahanie, and Suter Brook for condos and townhomes near the station, and Glenayre or Moody Centre for those stretching toward something detached or half-duplex.

Is Port Moody walkable?

Moody Centre and Inlet Centre genuinely are, with SkyTrain, shops, and Rocky Point in walking distance. The mountain and shoreline neighbourhoods are car-first by design.
