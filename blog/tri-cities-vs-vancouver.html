Tri-Cities vs Vancouver: where your housing dollar actually goes further.

By the Zada Group team · 6 minute read

Tri-Cities real estate exists in Vancouver's shadow, and for buyers that shadow is the opportunity. The same budget that buys a compact condo in Vancouver proper stretches to a townhouse, and sometimes a detached home, in Port Moody, Coquitlam, or Port Coquitlam. This is the comparison we walk clients through when they are deciding which side of Boundary Road their life should be on.

What the dollar buys.

The gap is not subtle. Vancouver's price per square foot runs well above the Tri-Cities across every housing type, which means each step out the Barnet or the Lougheed buys you bedrooms, outdoor space, and parking. For a growing family, the practical difference is often an entire housing type: condo money in Vancouver is townhouse money in Coquitlam, and townhouse money in Vancouver starts conversations about detached homes in Port Coquitlam.

The commute question, honestly.

The Evergreen Extension changed this calculation permanently. Moody Centre and Inlet Centre in Port Moody, and Burquitlam through Lafarge Lake in Coquitlam, are on the Millennium Line, and the West Coast Express runs to Waterfront on weekday mornings. If your work is downtown and you can live near a station, the Tri-Cities commute is a train ride with a book, not a crawl over a bridge. If you drive to a job site that moves around the region, you already know the answer depends on the week.

What you give up, and what you gain.

Honesty matters here. You give up walkable density, some restaurant depth, and proximity to the city's cultural core. You gain Rocky Point and the inlet, Buntzen and Sasamat within twenty minutes, newer housing stock for the money, and communities where daily life fits around kids instead of parking. Plenty of our clients keep one foot in the city for work and play and plant the family where the space is.

The investment angle.

Growth follows transit and land capacity, and the Tri-Cities have both: SkyTrain in place, SSMUH multiplying what each lot can hold, and municipal plans that anticipate substantial growth around the stations. Vancouver will always be Vancouver, but the structural drivers in the corridor from Burquitlam to Coquitlam Central are real and they have not gone anywhere.

If you are weighing the two, read our Port Moody neighbourhood guide next, then come tour with us for a weekend. The neighbourhoods argue their own case in person.

Frequently asked questions.

Is the commute from the Tri-Cities to downtown Vancouver realistic?

Yes, if you plan around transit. The Millennium Line serves Port Moody and Coquitlam, and the West Coast Express runs to Waterfront on weekday mornings. Living near a station is the key variable.

Which Tri-City is right for me?

Broadly: Port Moody for the inlet lifestyle and tight-knit feel, Coquitlam for range and transit access across many neighbourhood types, Port Coquitlam for value and family-sized lots. We tour clients through all three before narrowing.

Do Tri-Cities homes hold value?

No honest agent promises appreciation anywhere, but transit expansion, constrained land, and new multiplex zoning give the Tri-Cities structural support that few suburban markets can match.
