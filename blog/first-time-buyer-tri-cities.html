The first-time buyer's guide to the Tri-Cities.

By the Zada Group team · 8 minute read

First-time buyers in BC get more help than most realize, and in the Tri-Cities that help stretches further because the entry price points sit below Vancouver's. This is the practical version of the guide: the programs that actually matter, the taxes to plan for, and where we point first-time clients when the budget is real but not unlimited.

The programs worth knowing.

The big one provincially is the First Time Home Buyers' property transfer tax exemption. Under the current rules, a qualifying first-time buyer pays no property transfer tax on the first $500,000 of a home priced up to $835,000 fair market value, with a partial exemption available up to $860,000. On a typical Tri-Cities condo or townhouse purchase, that is thousands of dollars staying in your pocket at completion.

Federally, the First Home Savings Account lets you contribute up to $8,000 per year to a lifetime maximum of $40,000, with contributions tax-deductible and qualifying withdrawals tax-free. The RRSP Home Buyers' Plan allows withdrawals up to $60,000 per person for a first home, repaid over time. Used together and started early, these two accounts are the closest thing to free money the system offers a disciplined saver.

The costs people forget.

Beyond the down payment: inspection, appraisal if your lender requires one, legal fees and disbursements, adjustments at closing, moving costs, and for strata properties the move-in fee and a buffer for the first months. We tell clients to hold back a cushion beyond the down payment so completion week is boring instead of terrifying. Boring is the goal.

Where the value hides right now.

Entry-level value in the Tri-Cities concentrates in a few places: established condos in Coquitlam West and Burquitlam near the stations, townhomes in Port Coquitlam where the price per square foot is the region's quiet bargain, and older strata buildings in Port Moody's Inlet Centre where the location outruns the finishes. An older building with a healthy depreciation report and a boring strata council is often a better buy than a shiny one with thin contingency funds, and we read those documents with you before you fall in love.

Strategy beats speed.

Our buying practice is built on a simple sequence: real budget first, not pre-approval theatre; a pressure-tested wish list; documents reviewed before offers; and offers structured to win on terms, not just price. First homes are the start of a ladder. The clients we helped into starter condos years ago are the same people we help into detached homes today, and that is the outcome a first purchase should be designed around. Frequently asked questions.

How much is the BC first-time buyer property transfer tax exemption worth?

Qualifying first-time buyers pay no property transfer tax on the first $500,000 of a home priced up to $835,000 fair market value, with a partial exemption between $835,000 and $860,000 under current rules. Confirm eligibility with your lawyer or notary before completion.

What is the minimum down payment in Canada?

Five percent on the first $500,000 of the purchase price and ten percent on the portion above that, within insured mortgage limits. Twenty percent avoids mortgage default insurance entirely.

Where can a first-time buyer actually buy in the Tri-Cities?

Condos near Burquitlam and Coquitlam Central, townhomes in Port Coquitlam, and established strata in Port Moody's Inlet Centre are the usual starting points. The right answer depends on budget, timeline, and how long you plan to hold.
