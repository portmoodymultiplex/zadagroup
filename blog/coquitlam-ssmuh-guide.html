SSMUH in Coquitlam: what your lot allows now.

By the Zada Group team · 7 minute read

SSMUH in Coquitlam covers one of the largest supplies of eligible single-family land in Metro Vancouver. The city is big, the lots are often generous, and SkyTrain threads through it, which matters because transit proximity is what pushes unit counts up. If you own a detached home in Coquitlam, here is what the framework actually means for you.

The baseline rules.

Like every larger BC municipality, Coquitlam must permit small-scale multi-unit housing on most lots that used to be single-family only. In practical terms that is three to four units on a typical lot, and up to six on qualifying lots near frequent transit. The provincial legislation set the floor; the city's zoning implements it with the specifics on setbacks, height, and parking.

Why transit matters more in Coquitlam than almost anywhere.

The Evergreen Extension runs through Burquitlam, Coquitlam Central, Lincoln, and Lafarge Lake, and the West Coast Express stops at Coquitlam Central. Neighbourhoods like Burquitlam and parts of Coquitlam West sit within walking distance of stations, which is exactly the zone where higher unit counts and stronger land values concentrate. Two otherwise similar lots, one near a station and one a long drive from it, can carry very different redevelopment potential.

Neighbourhoods where the math gets interesting.

Coquitlam West and Burquitlam have been transitioning for years and the land market there already prices in redevelopment. The quieter opportunities tend to be in established areas like Austin Heights, Maillardville, Ranch Park, and Coquitlam East, where lots are large, streets are serviced, and the housing stock is often older than the land value justifies. That combination, an older house on valuable serviced land, is the classic multiplex candidate.

The homeowner's decision tree.

There are really four paths. Hold and do nothing, and the option value still accrues to you. Sell to a builder who will redevelop, and price the potential into your sale. Build yourself, which brings the highest potential return along with the most risk and capital. Or co-develop, partnering with a builder who brings capital and execution while you bring the land. Which path fits depends on your equity, your timeline, and how much disruption your family can absorb. We model all four for clients before recommending any of them.

For the provincial framework itself, start with our plain-language SSMUH guide. For the version specific to your lot, bring us your address and we will pull the numbers.

Frequently asked questions.

How many units does SSMUH allow in Coquitlam?

Three to four units on most previously single-family lots, and up to six on qualifying lots near frequent transit such as SkyTrain stations and high-frequency bus corridors.

Which Coquitlam neighbourhoods have the strongest multiplex potential?

Areas near the Evergreen Extension stations, including Burquitlam and Coquitlam West, plus established neighbourhoods with large serviced lots such as Austin Heights, Maillardville, and Ranch Park. Every lot is different, so a site-specific review matters.

Do I have to build to benefit?

No. The redevelopment potential is part of your property's value whether you build, sell to a builder, or simply hold. If you sell, that potential should be reflected in your pricing strategy.
