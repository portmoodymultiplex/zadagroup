The votes are in. So are the listings.

The numbers

If you've been waiting for the right time to move up in Port Moody, this might be it. Both elections are over. Port Moody picked a new mayor on October 17, and BC picked a new government a week later, so the guessing about who's in charge is done.

Meanwhile, the neighbourhoods that are usually impossible to buy into finally have homes for sale, and the bigger homes are the ones that came down the most. Here's what the numbers actually say, in regular human words.

The short version

Three things worth knowing

One

Bigger homes got cheaper faster. A typical Port Moody detached home dropped 8.4% in a year. Townhouses dropped 5.2%. That difference is the whole story for anyone moving up.

Two

Sellers are listing, buyers are slow. In September, 53 detached homes came on the market in Port Moody and Belcarra. 16 sold. You get to choose, and you get time to think.

Three

Both governments just changed. Port Moody elected a new mayor on October 17. BC elected a new government on October 24. Neither changes your purchase this fall, but both shape what gets built next.

Prices

What a typical Port Moody home costs now

Thirteen months of the benchmark price, September 2025 to September 2026. Pick a home type, then hover or tap any month.

$1,903,500Detached · September 2026

Detached: $2,080,500 in September 2025, $1,903,500 in September 2026. GVR reports that as an 8.4% decline.

Why September matters

The detached benchmark held between $1.94M and $1.98M from February through August, then dropped 3.0% in a single month. Townhouses did the same thing, down 4.7% in September alone. A drop like that after a flat spring and summer usually means sellers who waited all year are now pricing to sell.

How this compares

Port Moody detached fell 8.4% over the year. Across Greater Vancouver, the detached benchmark fell 7.3%. Coquitlam fell 6.6%. Port Moody is the most expensive detached market in the Tri-Cities, and right now it is correcting a little harder than its neighbours.

Benchmark price. The price GVR estimates for a typical home in an area, built from actual sales. It stays steady when one mansion or one teardown sells, which is why we use it instead of averages.

The upsize math

The step up got smaller

If you own now, the number that matters is not the price of the house you want. It is the gap between what yours sells for and what the next one costs. That gap shrank.

Gap = Port Moody detached benchmark minus the benchmark for your current home type. Before transaction costs.

What we are seeing on the ground

For years, the family neighbourhoods up the hill and along the water were the hardest places in Port Moody to buy into. A house would come up, draw a crowd, and be gone. If you missed it, you waited a year for the next one.

That has changed. In the first nine months of 2026, 403 detached homes were listed in Port Moody and Belcarra, up from 372 in the same stretch of 2025. Only 112 sold. Buyers are seeing homes on streets that rarely come up, and they are seeing more than one at a time.

What that means for you

Across Greater Vancouver, detached homes are selling at a rate GVR's own research links to falling prices. Their sales-to-active ratio for detached homes was 9.7% in September. Prices tend to come under pressure when that number stays below 12%, and rise when it stays above 20%.

In plain terms: more homes than buyers. That is when conditions, inspections and a real negotiation are back on the table, and when the price on the listing is a starting point.

Policy

Two elections in eight days

What actually changed, and what it means for someone buying a home this fall.

Disclosure. I managed Meghan Lahti's 2026 campaign for re-election as mayor. The results below are the City's numbers, not mine, and the read on each one is about what it means for buyers.

City of Port Moody · October 17

A new mayor and three new councillors

  • Haven Lurbiecki of Port Moody Voice was elected mayor, 5,400 votes to 4,854 for incumbent Meghan Lahti in the City's preliminary count. Turnout was about 39%.
  • Port Moody Voice is the first municipal party in the city's history. As a councillor, Lurbiecki was the only member of council to vote against the city's Official Community Plan.
  • Council: incumbents Callan Morrison, Amy Lubik and Kyla Knowles, plus newcomers Paul Lambert, Peter Thomasen and Alexandra Chan.

Our read

Expect the pace and shape of new development to be debated again, including the tower sites near SkyTrain. That affects what gets built years from now, not the homes on the market today. If new supply slows, established detached neighbourhoods do not get any bigger. That has historically worked in favour of people who own in them.

Province of BC · October 24

A new government in Victoria

  • The BC Conservatives won a majority. Lorne Doerkson is premier-designate, ending nine years of NDP government.
  • Locally, John McEwen (Conservative) won Port Moody-Burquitlam, 10,712 votes to 9,634 for NDP incumbent Rick Glumac in the preliminary count.
  • The housing platform promises faster approvals and puts density decisions back with local Official Community Plans. At the October 14 debate, Doerkson said he would keep the foreign buyers tax and the speculation and vacancy tax.
  • The province's multiplex zoning law (Bill 44) stays in force unless the legislature changes it. The party promised to repeal it in 2024, and Doerkson was reported committing to repeal at an October 20 campaign event, but the 2026 platform does not include repeal.

Our read

We don't think Bill 44 gets repealed outright. The platform's own language is that density decisions belong to local governments and residents, which points to more power for municipalities, not a reset. And even a full repeal wouldn't erase the zoning bylaws cities have already passed. Nothing here changes the cost of buying a home this fall, and if the change brings buyers back off the sidelines, today's selection is the first thing that disappears.

Our perspective

If you have been waiting to upsize, this is the window

The gap is the number to watch

Detached prices fell further than townhouse and condo prices. For a move-up buyer, that is the best version of a down market: you give up less on your sale than you save on your purchase.

Choice like this does not usually last

Neighbourhoods that used to see one or two listings a season have several at once. Buyers who move while inventory is high pick the house. Buyers who move after it clears take what is left.

You can negotiate again

With more listings than sales, conditions on financing and inspection are workable, and list price is a conversation. The work is knowing which homes are priced right and which are priced hopeful. That is what we do every day in Port Moody.

Sell and buy as one plan

Timing two transactions is the part that scares people. We map both sides together, so you know what yours will likely sell for before you write an offer on the next one.

The long view

Zoom out and it looks different

Change in the Port Moody detached benchmark over different time frames, to September 2026.

One year: -8.4%. Three years: -7.8%. Five years: +3.5%. Ten years: +37.1%. Past performance is not a forecast.

Port Moody detached homes are worth less than they were three years ago and more than they were ten years ago. Buying in a dip does not guarantee anything. It does mean you are buying at a price that has already come down, in a city where the supply of family homes on real lots is not growing.
