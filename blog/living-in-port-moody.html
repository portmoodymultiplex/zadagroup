Living in Port Moody: the honest local guide.

By the Zada Group team · 7 minute read

Living in Port Moody means explaining to friends that no, it is not Port Coquitlam, and yes, it really does feel different from the rest of the region. The City of the Arts is a small inlet town that happens to sit on a SkyTrain line, and the people who move here tend to stay. Here is the honest version of why, including the parts the tourism brochure skips.

The water and the woods.

The city wraps around the end of Burrard Inlet, and daily life wraps around Rocky Point Park: the pier, the pool, the ice cream lines, the shoreline trail that connects half the city car-free. Twenty minutes north sit Buntzen and Sasamat lakes, and Bert Flinn Park backs onto the mountain neighbourhoods. If your family measures wealth in evenings outside, Port Moody pays a dividend most cities cannot.

Brewers Row and the small-town gravity.

Murray Street's row of craft breweries gave the city an identity anchor, and the arts scene, the markets, and festival days at Rocky Point do the rest. The social fabric is real: you will run into people you know at the rec centre, and community issues get debated like family business. For some people that gravity is the entire point. If you prefer anonymity, you have been warned.

The practical file: commuting, schools, daily life.

Two SkyTrain stations, Moody Centre and Inlet Centre, plus the West Coast Express for the express downtown run. School District 43 serves the city, with catchments that families research closely, and the recreation complex, the library, and the shops at Suter Brook and Newport handle the everyday. The grocery-and-errands loop is genuinely easy; the specialty shopping trip still points at Coquitlam Centre or the city.

The trade-offs locals admit to.

Housing costs more than the rest of the Tri-Cities because supply is tight and demand is loyal. St. Johns Street carries more traffic than a town this size should. And change is coming visibly: Moody Centre's growth plans and the new multiplex zoning mean construction is part of the scenery for the next decade. We think that change is mostly opportunity, and we have written a whole guide on what it means for homeowners, but nobody should move here expecting the town to stay frozen.

If this sounds like your speed, the next step is a weekend here: coffee in Moody Centre, the shoreline trail, Rocky Point, a brewery flight, and a drive up the mountain. Then call us and tell us which neighbourhood pulled hardest. Our neighbourhood guide is the map for that conversation.

Frequently asked questions.

Is Port Moody a good place to raise a family?

It is built for it: parks and lakes, an engaged community, walkable pockets near the stations, and schools families actively choose. The constraint is housing cost, which runs above the neighbouring cities.

How long is the commute from Port Moody to downtown Vancouver?

Roughly forty minutes by SkyTrain from Moody Centre with a transfer, and the West Coast Express runs a fast direct trip to Waterfront on weekday mornings. Driving varies with the bridges and the hour.

Why is Port Moody called the City of the Arts?

The city has invested in its arts identity for decades, with galleries, festivals, and a cultural scene anchored around Rocky Point and Moody Centre that punches far above the population.
