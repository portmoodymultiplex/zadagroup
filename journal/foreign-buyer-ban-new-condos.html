Ottawa reopened new condos to foreign buyers. Here's the fine print.

SZ
Written by: Sheri Zada
Fact-checked, aggressively, by Dawar Zada, PREC*
Published Nov 12, 20268 minute read
The numbers

On November 5, the federal budget did two things to the foreign buyer ban at once. It extended the ban on existing homes for two more years, to January 1, 2029. And it carved out brand new homes, so people who aren't Canadian could buy newly built condos and houses again.

The headlines ran with "foreign buyers are back." Our phones lit up the same afternoon. Investors asking if this is the bottom for presales. Condo owners asking if their place just got more valuable. Buyers asking if they're about to get outbid by someone in Hong Kong or Seattle.

Short answer for all three: not yet, and maybe not much. Here in Metro Vancouver, the federal ban was only ever one of the walls. BC has its own, and nobody in Victoria has touched it.

This is the long answer. What changed, what didn't, why there are thousands of brand new condos sitting empty across the region (including a lot of them in our own backyard in Coquitlam), and what we'd actually do about it if you're buying, owning or investing right now.

If you read nothing else, read this

One

It isn't law yet. The budget is a proposal. Ottawa says legislation and regulations are coming "in short order," but there's no start date, and the current rules apply until there is one.

Two

BC's 20% tax still applies. A foreign buyer of a new condo in Metro Vancouver would still pay an extra 20% property transfer tax on top of everything else. That alone keeps most of them out.

Three

Resale is still off limits. If you own a condo, a townhouse or a house that someone has already lived in, foreign buyers still can't buy it until at least 2029.

Two governments, two sets of rules

Most of the confusion this week comes from treating these as one thing. They aren't. One decides whether a foreign buyer is allowed to buy. The other decides what it costs them.

Ottawa · Budget 2026

Who is allowed to buy

  • The ban today. Since January 1, 2023, most people who aren't Canadian citizens or permanent residents can't buy homes with up to three units, condos included, in cities and their surrounding regions. Metro Vancouver is fully covered. A buyer who breaks it, and anyone who knowingly helps them, can be fined up to $10,000, and a court can order the home sold.
  • The proposal. Keep the ban on existing homes until January 1, 2029, and exempt newly built homes.
  • Why. Ottawa's pitch is that most of the market stays reserved for Canadians while outside money helps fund new construction. Builders here and in Toronto have been asking for exactly this for two years.
  • What isn't answered yet. What counts as "new." Whether it covers presales, assignments, or a finished condo nobody has ever lived in. When it starts. None of that is in the budget.
  • Already exempt. Some work permit holders, students, refugees and others could already buy under conditions. That hasn't changed. If this is you, talk to a real estate lawyer before you write an offer.

Our read

This is real, but it's a door with no date on it. Until the legislation is written, nobody can tell you whether a specific unit qualifies, and we'd be very careful with anyone who says otherwise.

Victoria · BC rules

What it costs them

  • The 20% foreign buyer tax. Foreign nationals buying residential property in Metro Vancouver pay an additional property transfer tax of 20% of the fair market value. It applies to new homes and resale alike. The federal budget doesn't change it, because it can't. It's provincial law.
  • The speculation and vacancy tax. Foreign owners pay 3% of assessed value every year on a home that isn't lived in or rented out for at least six months. That rate is scheduled to go to 4% for 2027. (The City of Vancouver adds its own Empty Homes Tax on top, inside city limits only.)
  • No new-home tax break. BC waives property transfer tax on newly built homes up to $1.1 million, but only for Canadian citizens and permanent residents moving in. Foreign buyers pay full freight.
  • A new government. The BC Conservatives take over this month. At an October 14 debate, Premier-designate Lorne Doerkson said he'd keep the foreign buyer tax and the speculation and vacancy tax. Asked this week whether they'd exempt new homes from the 20%, the party said only that it's reviewing the federal changes.

Our read

This is the side that actually decides what happens in Metro Vancouver. Unless Victoria changes the 20%, the federal exemption is mostly symbolic here. Watch the new housing minister's first announcements. That's the real news.

If you qualify for both programs, the same condo carries no purchase taxes at all. That's a $227,500 head start over a foreign buyer on the exact same unit. Not everyone qualifies, and each program has its own rules and price caps, so check yours before you count on it.

The plain version. Ottawa says foreign buyers may come back for new homes. BC still charges them a quarter of a million dollars to do it. Very few people are going to take that deal.

Why there are so many empty new condos

Brand new condos in Metro Vancouver that are finished and still unsold, month by month, according to CMHC.

4,588Completed, unsold condos · Sep 2026

CMHC "completed and unabsorbed" condominium apartments, Vancouver CMA. A unit counts once construction is done and it has no buyer and no tenant. For comparison, the count was 1,262 in May 2024.

How we got here

A lot of these buildings were launched as presales around 2021 and 2022, when money was cheap and investors were buying condos on paper by the floor. Then rates went up, rents came down, and the math stopped working for a lot of those buyers. Some walked away from their deposits. Developers stopped selling the rest. Meanwhile 2025 delivered more finished condos than any year in the last 35, so the buildings kept arriving whether anyone was ready for them or not.

Research firm Real Property Data puts the value of finished, unsold condos across the region at about $4.4 billion, and more than 1,300 of them are in buildings finished at least two years ago. The big units are the hardest to move. Condos of 1,500 square feet or more had an unsold rate of 41.6%, compared to 12.2% for small ones.

Foreign buyers matter to this story because before the ban, they bought a meaningful share of exactly this product. In 2020, non-residents owned about 13.4% of BC's newer condos, more than in any other province Statistics Canada studied. That's the buyer the builders are hoping to get back.

Closer to home

Coquitlam had 814 finished, empty new condos in August, the highest number in 36 years according to SFU's Andy Yan, and a lot of them are in Burquitlam along the SkyTrain. Port Moody had 73. Port Coquitlam had seven. Roughly three quarters of Coquitlam's unsold units are priced between $500,000 and $1 million.

Two government plans aimed at this inventory came out of 2026. In June, Ottawa and the previous BC government announced a program to buy up to 2,200 unsold condos below cost and turn them into rent-to-own homes. During the fall campaign, the BC NDP also promised a new tax on condos left unsold for more than a year. The NDP lost, so the unsold condo tax is off the table. What happens to the rent-to-own buyout is now up to the new government, and it hasn't said.

Unabsorbed. CMHC's word for a finished unit that isn't sold, rented, or held back by the developer. It's a count of empty, brand new homes, not of resale listings.

What we'd do right now

Same news, three very different situations.

If you're buying

The number of finished, unsold new condos in Metro Vancouver is at a record high, which makes this a genuinely good time to buy one. The foreign buyer news doesn't change that. Developers holding finished inventory are paying interest, strata fees and property taxes on every empty unit every month. That's leverage, and it's yours.

Ask for price, but also ask for the things that don't show up in the advertised price: parking, storage, closing cost credits, upgraded finishes, a rate buydown. If you're a first-time buyer, run the GST rebate and the newly built home exemption before you compare a new condo to a resale one. On new product, they can be worth more than the discount.

If you own a condo

Foreign buyers still can't buy your home until at least 2029, so this news doesn't add buyers to your resale condo. What it does tell you is who you're competing with. In Burquitlam especially, your listing sits next to brand new units with full warranties, developer incentives, and in some cases no purchase tax for a first-time buyer.

If you're selling, price for that comparison, not for what your neighbour got in 2022. If you're holding, that's fine too, but if the place is empty, make sure you know where you stand on the speculation and vacancy tax and your strata's rental rules. Renting it out is often the cheapest way to wait.

If you invest

Don't buy anything on the theory that foreign money is about to flood back in. Until BC moves on the 20%, it isn't. If Victoria does exempt new homes for principal residences, the new-build side of the market gets a wider pool of buyers, and that's when this becomes worth paying attention to.

Until then, the opportunity is the one in front of you: developers who need to sell, and a new provincial government that has said it wants housing built faster. If you're building missing-middle homes, a new multiplex unit should count as "new" under the federal change. Whether that helps depends entirely on what BC does next, so plan your exit around today's buyers, not tomorrow's.

Our honest read

We understand why builders wanted this. Presales are how condo towers get financed, and when presales stall, the towers that should be breaking ground in 2027 don't. CMHC says condo apartment starts in Metro Vancouver fell another 40% in the first half of this year. Fewer starts now means fewer homes in four years, no matter who buys them.

We also understand why people are skeptical. Opening new homes to foreign buyers while the region sits on thousands of empty ones looks a lot like a rescue for the people who built too many of the wrong thing. Both of those can be true at the same time.

What we're sure of is this: for anyone living in Metro Vancouver, nothing about this week's news changes the deal in front of you today. The prices are what they are, the inventory is what it is, and the negotiating power is still sitting with the buyer. The thing that would actually change the picture is a decision in Victoria about that 20%. When it comes, we'll write about it here.

In the meantime, if you want to know whether a specific building, unit or lot fits any of this, ask us. That's the fun part.
