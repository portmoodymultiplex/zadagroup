How she gets a home seen

Every Zada Group listing gets treated like a product launch. Its own website, photo and video direction, copy written from scratch, and print pieces for the neighbourhood. Boutique, not cookie-cutter.

Then come the ads. Sheri runs our Meta campaigns herself: custom audiences built from our own database, lookalikes of past buyers, retargeting anyone who watched the video or opened the listing site, and creative tested in rounds until one wins. Sellers get in front of buyers who are actually shopping, not just people who double-tap.

Our database is her favourite spreadsheet. When a home is about to hit the market, she already has a good idea who has been waiting for it, and when a buyer tells us what they want, she is the one keeping an eye out.

And she shows up. Community events planned with the City of Coquitlam, fundraisers, disaster response efforts. People remember who showed up, and that reach comes with every listing we take.

The rules get patched every election. She reads the patch notes.

In November 2023 the province introduced Bill 44, opening most single-family lots in BC to three to six homes instead of one.

Sheri and Dawar moved first. Dawar was the first homeowner in Port Moody to move a multiplex application forward, with an 11,000 sq ft lot approved for four beautiful homes: livable, good value and right for the neighbourhood. Our investor clients followed, and we have been involved in almost every SSMUH application in Port Moody since.

Bill 44 is one example. Transit-oriented areas, new amenity charges, a new Official Community Plan: every time the rules change, we read the fine print, work out what it means for a specific lot, and get our investors there early.

That's what happens when you pay attention.

Give me the most complicated idea in the room. I'll make it make sense to everyone else.

The best childhoods are full of magic

Sheri is a full-time mom who believes the best childhoods are the ones filled with magic. You can catch her at all hours of the night cutting brie into a horror movie mask, turning waffles into bunnies and engineering a spider web out of a sandwich.

She brings the same energy to a lunch box that she brings to a council agenda package. Way too much, on purpose.

She lives in Port Moody with Dawar, their three kids and Bear, a white Samoyed who has never once been left out of a family photo.
