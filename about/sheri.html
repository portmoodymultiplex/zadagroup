She reads the fine print so you don't have to

Sheri runs communications and community relations for Zada Group, the Port Moody real estate team she built with her husband, Dawar Zada, PREC*.

She reads the whole agenda package, attachments and appendices included, down to the staff recommendations that never make the news. Then she explains it in plain English. Usually with a chart. She really loves a chart.

The point of all that reading: our clients never get blindsided by a decision made at City Hall or in Victoria.

The rules get patched every election. She reads the patch notes.

In the last few years the province has rewritten how BC cities plan for housing, from small-scale multi-unit housing to transit-oriented areas, and it will keep changing. Port Moody has two SkyTrain stations in the middle of all of it. In October 2026 it also got a new council and a new provincial government, one week apart.

In gaming terms, the rules keep getting patched, and most buyers never read the patch notes. They find out what changed for their street after they have already bought on it.

Sheri reads the patch notes. All of them. Between the market, the legislation, the municipalities and the government agencies behind them, there is a whole picture, and our clients get all of it before they sign. Real estate as one connected plan, not a transaction followed by four years of hoping.

For investors, that is the difference between buying a lot and buying what that lot can become.

My job is making brilliant, busy people legible to the outside world.
