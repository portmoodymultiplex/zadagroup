The rules get patched every election. She reads the patch notes.

In November 2023 the province introduced Bill 44, which opened most single-family lots in BC to three to six homes instead of one. Bills 46 and 47 came with it, changing how cities charge for growth and how much density belongs near transit.

Sheri and Dawar moved fast. Dawar was the first homeowner in Port Moody to move a multiplex application forward under the new rules, on our own lot, and our investor clients used the same head start on theirs.

Fast forward to today. There is a new government in Victoria, and its premier-designate, Lorne Doerkson, has talked about repealing Bill 44. His party promised it in 2024. His 2026 platform doesn't. What it does say is that density decisions belong to local governments.

Our read: the rules aren't going anywhere, but cities are about to get a lot more say over them. A full repeal is unlikely, and even one wouldn't erase the zoning bylaws cities have already passed. We're not worried. Our clients used the window while it was open, and we're already watching for whatever comes next.

That's what happens when you pay attention.
